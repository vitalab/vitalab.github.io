# RLCR

**RLCR** (Reinforcement Learning with Calibration Rewards) trains reasoning models to output a confidence estimate along with their answer, adding a Brier score term to the correctness reward. This improves calibration without hurting accuracy.

![](/article/images/RLCR/figure1.png)

## Background

Most reasoning models are trained with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR): a process in which a model is fine-tuned with binary rewards only considering correctness.
This rewards models regardless of whether they are confident or guessing.

RLVR can often lead to models becoming over-confident, even if they were previously well calibrated.

RLVR's reward is generally simply:

$$R_{correctness}(\hat{y}, y) = \mathbb{1}_{\hat{y}\equiv y}$$

This paper attempts to develop a method to both improve correctness and calibration.

## Method

In this method, models are prompted to provide reasoning chains followed by an answer and a confidence estimate. 
The reward used to train them is then:

$$R_{RLCR}(\hat{y}, q, y) = \mathbb{1}_{\hat{y}\equiv y} - (q - \mathbb{1}_{\hat{y}\equiv y})^2$$

This reward format incentivizes correctness but penalizes outputs which are incorrect with high confidence or correct answers with low confidence.

![](/article/images/RLCR/reward.png)

The second term introduced in the reward is the Brier score, an example of a proper scoring rule. 
A scoring rule measures the quality of a confidence estimate. 
In the case of binary outcomes, it is a function $S: \mathbb{R} \times \{0, 1\} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ that maps a confidence estimate $q$ and an outcome $a$ to a scalar score. 
A scoring rule is proper if its expected value is minimized by confidence scores that match the true outcome probability:

$$\mathbb{E}_{a \sim p(a)} S(p(a), a) \le \mathbb{E}_{a \sim p(a)} S(q, a) \forall q$$

Examples of proper scores include Logarithmic score: $S(q, a) = -a \log q - (1-a) \log(1-q)$, Brier score: $S(q, a) = (q - a)^2$, spherical score: $S(q, a) = -\frac{aq + (1-a)(1-q)}{\sqrt{q^2 + (1-q)^2}}$, etc. They are all minimized when confidences $q$ match true probability $p(a=1)$.

This relies on _Theorem 1_, which proves the calibration term does not come with a cost in accuracy, if the proper scoring rule used is bounded and satisfies $S(p, 1) - S(p, 0) > \lambda$ for some $\lambda$. Logarithmic score is not bounded, Brier score is.

## Experiments

**Setup**

- Model: Qwen2.5-7B, trained with GRPO, no KL regularization term
- Output format: reasoning, answer, analysis of uncertainty, confidence score $q$
- Training datasets: HotpotQA (multi-hop factual QA) and Big-Math (math reasoning)
- Evaluation: in-domain, plus out-of-distribution (6 datasets for HotpotQA, 5 for Math)
- Metrics: accuracy, AUROC, Brier score and ECE

**Baselines**

- Base model, and RLVR alone
- RLVR + BCE classifier, and RLVR + Brier classifier (separate model that predicts confidence)
- RLVR + probe (linear probe on hidden states)
- Answer probability (confidence taken from token probabilities)

![](/article/images/RLCR/results.png)

RLCR matches RLVR accuracy and improves calibration by a large margin, especially out-of-distribution.

Notable finding:

- Using verbalized confidence $q$ to select responses from an ensemble can lead to an increase in performance.

An ablation study is also performed to see what drives calibration gains:

![](/article/images/RLCR/ablation.png)

Both analysis and RLCR contribute to calibration increases, with RLCR w/o analysis being a drop-in replacement for RLVR without increasing compute.

## Interpretation and conclusion

With this method, reasoning models are able to improve both on accuracy and calibration jointly. 
Models are prompted to verbalize uncertainty, with authors demonstrating that reasoning about uncertainty improves calibration.

## Sidenote

[OpenJev-RLCD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.38850) (Gao & Wang, 2026) also uses the Brier score as an RL reward, but scores the answer distribution read from the model's logits instead of a verbalized confidence. It cites RLCR as concurrent work.
